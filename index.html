The architecture

One line decides everything: what is welded on, and what is free to move.

Anything to the left of it is permanent the moment a token is minted, and a future game can only honour it, reinterpret it, or ignore it. Anything to the right can be rebalanced, re-skinned or replaced forever. The discipline is simply keeping as little as possible on the left.

Immutable · welded on

What the token stores

Chosen once, at mint, for the lifetime of the asset.

  • Token standard — ERC-1155, for batch transfers and gas efficiency
  • Core identity — weapon_class / item_type
  • rarity_tier and a unique ID
  • Base visual reference — an IPFS hash, nothing more
  • Ownership and transfer rules
  • Light abstract tiers — fire_rate_tier, damage_multiplier_tier, special_tag

Risk: once minted these cannot be changed. A future game's only options are to honour them, reinterpret them, or ignore them entirely.

Updatable · free to move

What lives outside it

A stats and compatibility layer, or ordinary game logic.

  • Exact numbers — damage, fire rate, recoil, handling
  • Balance, tuning and every future rebalance
  • Art, animation and visual effects, per game
  • Abilities, perks and interactions
  • Engine-specific data for side-scroller, forward-scroller or 3D FPS
  • A map from token_id → current values

Why it is safe: anything here can be corrected after launch. A balance mistake costs a patch, not the asset.

The divider is the architecture. Move a number to the left and you have promised it forever; leave it on the right and every future title can still use the item.

Recommended structure · guns example

Three layers, each replaceable except the first.

Layer 1 · NFT

Pure ownership token plus core identityHolds who owns it, what kind of thing it is, its rarity and its base art reference. It never learns what a bullet does.

Layer 2 · Stats

Updatable stats & compatibility contractMaps token ID to current numbers, on-chain or in game logic. Rebalancing happens here, and it can happen forever.

Layer 3 · Game

Each game supplies its own art, animation and balanceA side-scroller, a forward-scroller and a future 3D FPS can all read the same token without any engine-specific data ever being baked into it.

Design compromises

Every choice here trades permanence against freedom.

ChoiceUpsideDownside
Extremely generic NFT Maximum future flexibility Feels slightly less "special" in Game 1
More stats baked on-chain Stronger feeling of permanence Locks balance forever; future games may have to nerf or ignore it
Explicit "no future guarantees" language Protects against community harassment Slightly lower launch hype and FOMO
Extra contracts and interfaces now Far less technical debt later Higher upfront complexity

The pledge

"Utility is only guaranteed in the first game. Future titles may or may not support these NFTs. There is no obligation or timeline."

Said plainly, in the same words, everywhere a buyer might look. Promising future compatibility is the one mistake that cannot be patched later — it converts every future design decision into a debt owed to people who bought expecting something else.

Community & anti-griefing

The rules that keep a bonus from becoming a debt.

  • STATE ITPut the no-guarantees line in the contract comments, on the site and in the whitepaper — the same sentence in all three.
  • BUILD ITMake Game 1 excellent and complete on its own. The item must be worth owning if no second game ever ships.
  • NEVERNever market future compatibility as certain — not in a tweet, not in a Discord reply.
  • OPTIONALA separate Compatibility Registry contract that future games may opt into, if and when they choose to.

Storage & frontend

Storage

  • Images and media on IPFS, or Arweave where stronger permanence is wanted.
  • Never store critical data on private servers that can disappear.
  • Metadata JSON stays minimal, standardised, and pinned on IPFS as well.

Frontend

  • A simple static site that reads on-chain data — Vercel, Netlify or Cloudflare Pages.
  • Keep it lightweight and fast so it never becomes a dependency for the NFTs themselves.
  • If this site vanished tomorrow, every token would be unaffected.

Other considerations

Known costs, listed before they are paid.

  • Centralised off-chain dataStats or images on private servers can vanish, taking the item's meaning with them.
  • Upgradeable proxy contractsAdd complexity and can reduce community trust in the permanence being claimed.
  • Marketplace fragmentationEach game may run its own marketplace, splitting liquidity.
  • Art-style translation costThe same weapon must be redrawn to fit each game's visual language.
  • Legal & licensing languageRights must be explicit, including the absence of rights to future titles.
  • Gas & batch efficiencyAn ERC-1155 advantage worth keeping, especially for multi-item transfers.
  • Wallet UXAccount abstraction matters for players who are not crypto users.
  • Regulatory uncertaintyNFTs are treated as securities in some jurisdictions.
  • Long-term maintenanceAny shared stats or compatibility contract is a permanent upkeep commitment.
